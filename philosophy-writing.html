Teaching Philosophy #5

Writing

As a writing teacher, I value both the process- and product-oriented approaches to writing. In this, I help students learn and practice utilizing the writing process as well as producing a finished, intelligible piece. This also helps them avoid relying on artificial intelligence to do the writing process for them. In process writing, I believe that writing instruction should balance the development of ideas, organization, and language accuracy rather than focusing only on grammar. Students need opportunities to experience a variety of writing genres among both approaches (e.g., doing different kinds of academic writing, creative writing, projects, etc.). Additionally, I believe they should actively write during every lesson. Overall, the goal for my writing classes is for my students to communicate their ideas effectively in their formal and informal writing, through both process and product.

Hence, these ideals are realized in my classroom through process-writing and product-writing instruction. Process-writing instruction looks like building students’ confidence through scaffolded writing tasks and opportunities for revision; they complete multiple drafts of the same writing project. Moreover, I help them develop organization and planning skills through activities such as outlining, graphic organizers, and the analysis of example texts. I conduct modeling activities which demonstrate how effective writers make decisions about audience, purpose, and organization. To develop writing finished products, I provide consistent rubrics in which students know exactly what they’ll be graded on. As part of my rubrics, I grade holistic idea development and content, organization, vocabulary, and accuracy. I also give effective and timely feedback on how they can improve their content, organization, and language use to guide them to the final product. Finally, I support their independent writing development through peer review activities and revision activities. In sum, I believe that students should develop the skills and confidence in class that are necessary to become independent writers beyond the classroom.
