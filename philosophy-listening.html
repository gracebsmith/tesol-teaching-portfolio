Teaching Philosophy #2

Listening

I see listening as more than passive reception; it is learning how to make and infer meaning from speech. Some of the underlying theories that influence my philosophy about teaching listening include focusing on listening skills and utilizing both metacognitive and task-based listening activities during my instruction. I am also influenced by the Pedagogical Model for Second Language Listening, specifically the critical and intertextual dimensions. This model reminds me that listening involves more than understanding words; it also includes evaluating messages, drawing on prior knowledge, and making connections between ideas, texts, and experiences. As a result, I view listening as an active and meaning-making process rather than a purely receptive skill.

In my classes, I help students to develop strategic awareness by consciously choosing strategies that match their goals and challenges with English and by thinking critically about what works best for them. I aim to make the core listening skills the basis of my syllabus, including listening for details, global understanding, and main ideas, as well as listening selectively, inferring, and predicting. These skills provide a foundation that students can apply across a variety of listening situations. Task-based instruction, where students actively practice listening through activities such as note-taking, discussion, and information-gap tasks, serves as a framework for helping students develop and apply these skills. Additionally, metacognitive activities, such as planning before listening and reflecting afterward, support task-based activities by helping students recognize what contributed to their understanding, monitor their progress, and identify what they might do differently the next time they encounter a similar listening task.
