Research

Selected projects in applied linguistics, TESOL, and accessible communication.

My research explores how language works in real-world contexts and how communication can better serve diverse communities. These projects have strengthened my skills in research design, corpus analysis, and connecting linguistic findings to practical questions.

Applied linguisticsLING 620 · 2026

Accessibility of Community Flyers: A Content Analysis of Social Service Flyers in Utah County

Co-authored with Julia Jensen and Sage Uchytil, this study examines how linguistically and visually accessible local social-service flyers are for people with lower levels of English proficiency. The team analyzed 30 community flyers to identify features that can help or hinder readers’ understanding and to inform clearer public-facing materials.

Authority in Words: Stance Modal Verbs and Directives in Presidents Nelson and Benson’s Talks

This mentored study compares stance modals and directive language in 41 talks by President Russell M. Nelson and 27 talks by President Ezra Taft Benson. Corpus analysis found no significant difference in their overall modal-verb use; President Benson used collective directives more often, while President Nelson favored personal directives.

Independent undergraduate research · Mentored by Dr. Brent Green
