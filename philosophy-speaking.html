Teaching Philosophy #3

Speaking

The main goal for my speaking classes is for students to be intelligible and communicate their ideas effectively. My philosophy includes focusing on four major areas: accuracy, fluency, appropriacy, and authenticity. Fluency is the ability to communicate ideas at a smooth, conversational pace, while accuracy involves using grammar, vocabulary, and language structures correctly. Moreover, appropriacy in my language instruction includes practicing different registers, pragmatics, and situations in which students will use English. Authenticity includes selecting materials that native English speakers would typically encounter in daily life, allowing learners to practice real-world language use and prepare for communication beyond the classroom. Additionally, I believe pronunciation instruction should prioritize intelligibility rather than achieving a “correct” accent. Students should be understood clearly by a wide range of listeners while maintaining their linguistic identities.

In classroom application, these ideas are reflected through a variety of communicative speaking tasks designed to develop different aspects of speaking proficiency. During role-play activities, students either assume different roles and exchange information to practice fluency, or they prepare and perform scripts to practice accuracy. Role plays also provide opportunities to practice appropriacy by requiring students to adjust their language according to different social situations, levels of formality, and communicative purposes. I incorporate regular instruction and practice with pronunciation so that students can communicate clearly and confidently in different contexts. Finally, drawing on the ELC’s LATs and ACTFL’s proficiency frameworks, I utilize assessments that examine text type, content, and accuracy. I use assessments that focus on what students can do with language and their ability to communicate meaningfully in authentic situations.
