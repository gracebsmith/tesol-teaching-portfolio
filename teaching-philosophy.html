02 / Teaching and learning

Teaching Philosophy

My teaching begins with the people in the room and the language they want to use beyond it.

I believe effective TESOL teaching brings together clear outcomes, meaningful language use, and relationships grounded in respect. Students learn best when they understand what they are working toward, see how lessons connect to their goals, and have a voice in how they practice.

01

Purposeful and responsive instruction

I make learning outcomes the framework for course and lesson decisions. I share those goals with students, invite their input where it can support learning, and use their feedback to adjust activities, materials, and pacing. I aim for a classroom climate that is warm and encouraging while keeping learning focused and appropriately challenging.

02

Communication with meaning

Students need opportunities to use English for real purposes. I connect listening, speaking, reading, and writing to authentic tasks, and I teach strategies that help learners understand, express, and evaluate ideas. In speaking, I prioritize intelligibility and effective communication while respecting students’ linguistic identities.

03

Confidence and independence

I scaffold practice so students can take on increasingly complex work. Readers benefit from engaging, level-appropriate texts, choice, and reflection on their strategies. Writers need time to plan, draft, receive useful feedback, revise, and produce a finished piece for a real audience and purpose.

04

Language in context

Grammar, vocabulary, and culture are connected parts of communication. I want students to understand not only what language forms mean, but also how and when people use them. Meaningful practice, repeated encounters, and attention to context can help learners communicate with accuracy, confidence, and intercultural awareness.

My goal is for students to leave class more prepared to use English independently for their academic, professional, and personal purposes.
