Teaching Philosophy #4

Reading

The goal for my reading classes is for my students to become engaged and independent readers. I value the nine curricular principles for EAP reading instruction because they emphasize integrating regular reading practice into instruction, using interesting and level-appropriate reading materials, and giving students choices in what they read. I believe that reading instruction should connect to students’ background knowledge and include structured pre, during, and post reading tasks. Additionally, I believe students need opportunities to experience comprehension success and they should read during every lesson. Engaged readers should read with a clear purpose, read fluently enough to focus on meaning, use what they read to develop comprehension, and consciously apply reading strategies. Encouraging student motivation is also important because they are more likely to invest effort when they understand the purpose and value of reading.

In my classroom, these ideas are realized through vocabulary instruction, comprehension practice, fluency activities, and metacognitive strategy instruction. I build students’ recognition vocabulary through explicit instruction and using level-appropriate words. I develop comprehension skills through activities such as elaborative interrogation and comprehension monitoring to encourage students to think about their thinking. To develop fluency, I use rate-building activities in which students reread a passage during timed intervals, check comprehension, and set individual fluency goals. I also give effective feedback on how they can improve their fluency. I conduct think-aloud activities which model how effective readers prepare, plan, monitor, and evaluate their reading. Finally, I support motivation through level-appropriate and interesting texts and student choice, especially in extensive reading. Overall, I believe that students should develop the skills and confidence in class that are necessary to become independent readers beyond the classroom.
